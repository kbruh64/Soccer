Hall of Fame

Twenty eight inductees across five wings. Chosen on what the record shows rather than on affection, which is why three of them lost the final they are here for, and why an entire wing never played a match.

This is not FIFA's hall of fame. FIFA does not operate one, and nothing on this page carries any official standing. It is an editorial selection made by this project, and it is argued for rather than asserted: every inductee below is given a citation you can check. Disagree with it and you are probably right about someone.

The inner circle

Six players whose absence from any serious list would be the argument, rather than their presence on it.

Inductee 01

Pelé

Brazil, 1958 to 1970

The only player to win three World Cups, and he was seventeen for the first of them, scoring twice in the final. Twelve tournament goals across four appearances. No other career spans the arrival of television, the arrival of money, and the arrival of the modern game itself.

Inductee 02

Diego Maradona

Argentina, 1982 to 1994

Took possession of one tournament more completely than any player before or since. Five goals in 1986, including both against England inside four minutes: one with his hand, one after running through half the team.

Inductee 03

Johan Cruyff

Netherlands, 1974

Inducted for a tournament he lost. He won the Golden Ball in 1974 and the side he led changed what every team afterwards tried to do with the ball. Influence is not the same as a medal, and here it counts for more.

Inductee 04

Franz Beckenbauer

West Germany, 1966 to 1990

Lifted the trophy as captain in 1974 and won it again as manager in 1990, which only two other men have done. He also more or less invented the position he played, stepping out of defence to start attacks because nobody had told him not to.

Inductee 05

Zinedine Zidane

France, 1998 to 2006

Two headers from corners in the 1998 final, from a player who had been sent off in the group stage. Won the Golden Ball in 2006 and was then sent off in the final, which was the last match he ever played. Both halves belong in the citation.

Inductee 06

Lionel Messi

Argentina, 2006 to 2026

Six World Cups across twenty years, twenty one goals, the Golden Ball in both 2014 and 2022, and the trophy at the fifth attempt. He briefly held the all time scoring record in 2026 before a Frenchman fourteen years younger took it off him.

The builders

Six people who never scored a World Cup goal and without whom there would be no World Cup to score in. One of them is here for a shoebox and one for a sculpture.

1904 to 1906

Robert Guérin

France, first president

A journalist of twenty eight who wrote the founding charter and then travelled to London to persuade the Football Association to sign it. They told him the idea was not worth discussing. He founded FIFA anyway.

1918 to 1921

Carl Hirschman

Netherlands, secretary

Kept FIFA in existence from an Amsterdam office through the First World War and the years after it, largely funding the work himself while its members were at war with each other. Never elected president, and the federation would not have survived without him.

1921 to 1954

Jules Rimet

France, president

Thirty three years in office, the longest of anyone. Pushed the World Cup through against sustained European objection, and membership grew from twenty associations to eighty five while he held the post. The first trophy carried his name for a reason.

Wartime custody

Ottorino Barassi

Italy, federation secretary

Removed the trophy from a bank vault in Rome before occupying forces could requisition it and kept it in a shoebox under his bed for the duration of the Second World War. Every winner since 1950 has lifted a cup that exists because of this.

1961 to 1974

Stanley Rous

England, president

Rewrote the Laws of the Game as a referee, reorganising them into the form still recognisable today. Lost the presidency in 1974 because he would not move quickly enough on South Africa, and the record has to carry both facts.

1971

Silvio Gazzaniga

Italy, sculptor

Designed the replacement trophy after Brazil kept the original, choosing two figures holding up the earth. Every champion since 1974 has been photographed with his work, and almost nobody knows his name.

The women's game

The competition only began in 1991, so every player here built something rather than inherited it. One of them has won more world player awards than any footballer of either gender.

Inductee 13

Marta

Brazil, 2003 to 2023

Six world player of the year awards, which is more than any footballer of either gender has won. Seventeen goals at Women's World Cups, also a record for either gender. Six tournaments, and never a winner's medal, which changes nothing about the citation.

Inductee 14

Mia Hamm

United States, 1991 to 2004

Won the first Women's World Cup in 1991 at nineteen and the 1999 tournament in front of ninety thousand people at the Rose Bowl. Took the first two world player of the year awards when FIFA finally created one for women in 2001.

Inductee 15

Birgit Prinz

Germany, 1994 to 2011

Three consecutive world player awards from 2003 to 2005, and World Cups in 2003 and 2007. Germany did not concede a single goal across the whole of the 2007 tournament, which no other champion has managed.

Inductee 16

Homare Sawa

Japan, 1993 to 2015

At the 2011 tournament, months after the Tohoku earthquake, she won the Golden Ball, the Golden Boot and the World Cup, at thirty two, in her fifth attempt. Then the world player of the year award. No other player has taken all four in one year.

Inductee 17

Abby Wambach

United States, 2001 to 2015

One hundred and eighty four international goals, which was a world record for either gender when she retired in 2015. Won the World Cup in her final tournament at thirty five, having spent four previous ones losing it.

Inductee 18

Aitana Bonmatí

Spain, 2017 to present

Golden Ball at the 2023 World Cup as Spain won its first title, then world player of the year in consecutive years. Spain held the men's and women's world titles simultaneously in 2026, and her generation is the reason the second half of that sentence exists.

The goalkeepers

A separate wing because the position is judged on a different scale, and because the awards that decide these arguments were not designed with goalkeepers in mind.

One tournament, and then nothing like it again

Six players inducted for four weeks of work. None of them were ever this good again, and it did not matter. Drag or scroll the rail sideways.

1950

Alcides Ghiggia

Uruguay

Scored once in the decisive match and a crowd recorded at 173,850 stopped making noise. He later said only three people have silenced the Maracanã, and named himself last.

1954

Sándor Kocsis

Hungary

Eleven goals in five matches, including two hat tricks, for the best team never to win it. Hungary had not lost in four years and lost the one match that counted.

1958

Just Fontaine

France

Thirteen goals in six matches, in boots borrowed from a teammate because his own had split. Seventeen tournaments have gone by and nobody has come within two of it.

1966

Eusébio

Portugal

Nine goals, four of them in one quarter final after Portugal had gone three down to North Korea. Third place, and Portugal did not better it for forty years.

1990

Salvatore Schillaci

Italy

Started the tournament as a substitute nobody outside Italy had heard of and finished it as top scorer with six. He scored one more international goal in his entire career.

1994

Oleg Salenko

Russia

Five goals in one match against Cameroon, still the record. Six in three matches, a shared Golden Boot, elimination in the group stage, and never another appearance for his country.

What the awards actually say

FIFA has called its world player prize three different things since 1991. Counting across all three names is the only way to compare eras honestly.

6

Marta, world player awards

More than any footballer of either gender. Five of them consecutively between 2006 and 2010, and a sixth in 2018.

8

Messi, world player awards

Across all three names the prize has carried: FIFA World Player of the Year, the FIFA Ballon d'Or, and The Best FIFA Men's Player.

1

Goalkeepers with a Ballon d'Or

Lev Yashin, in 1963, and nobody in the sixty odd years since. The award is not built to notice what goalkeepers do.
