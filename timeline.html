Twenty three tournaments

From thirteen teams in Montevideo to forty eight across three countries. Ninety six years, two cancelled by war, and one player from each edition who is the reason people still talk about it.

Tournament played Cancelled

A note on the photographs. These are portraits of the players, not pictures taken at the tournament beside them. Several were taken decades later, because that is what exists under a free licence. Where a photograph does show the tournament itself it is described as such in the caption. All images are from Wikimedia Commons and are credited in the footer.

1930

Uruguay

Uruguay

4 goals to 2 v Argentina, Montevideo

Golden BootGuillermo Stábile, Argentina, 8

José Nasazzi

Uruguay's captain lifted the first trophy in front of a home crowd at the Centenario, in a stadium finished days before the tournament began.

1934

Italy

Italy

2 goals to 1 after extra time v Czechoslovakia, Rome

Golden BootOldrich Nejedly, Czechoslovakia, 5

Giuseppe Meazza

Italy's inside forward, and the first man to win the tournament twice. The San Siro carries his name.

1938

France

Italy

4 goals to 2 v Hungary, Paris

Golden BootLeônidas, Brazil, 7

Leônidas

Brazil's top scorer, rested for the semifinal his coach assumed they would win. They lost. It was the last World Cup for twelve years.

1942 and 1946 cancelled

No tournament was staged. FIFA continued to exist on paper from Zurich, and the trophy spent the war in a shoebox under the bed of an Italian official in Rome.

1950

Brazil

Uruguay

2 goals to 1 v Brazil, decisive match, Rio de Janeiro

Golden BootAdemir, Brazil, 9

Alcides Ghiggia

Scored the goal at the Maracanã that silenced a crowd recorded at 173,850. The only tournament decided by a final group rather than a final.

1954

Switzerland

West Germany

3 goals to 2 v Hungary, Bern

Golden BootSándor Kocsis, Hungary, 11

Fritz Walter

Captained a side nobody expected past a Hungary team that had not lost a match in four years. West Germany had trailed 2 goals to 0 inside eight minutes.

1958

Sweden

Brazil

5 goals to 2 v Sweden, Stockholm

Golden BootJust Fontaine, France, 13

Pelé

Seventeen years old, and scored twice in the final. Brazil's first title, and the first won by a South American side in Europe.

1962

Chile

Brazil

3 goals to 1 v Czechoslovakia, Santiago

Golden BootSix players tied on 4

Garrincha

Carried Brazil through the knockout rounds after Pelé was injured in the group stage. Born with legs of different lengths, and impossible to defend against.

1966

England

England

4 goals to 2 after extra time v West Germany, Wembley

Golden BootEusébio, Portugal, 9

Bobby Moore

Wiped his muddy hands on the velvet before shaking the Queen's, then lifted the trophy. England's only title, and the only final decided by a goal still disputed.

1970

Mexico

Brazil

4 goals to 1 v Italy, Mexico City

Golden BootGerd Müller, West Germany, 10

Carlos Alberto

Captain, and scorer of the fourth in the final after a move that ran the length of the pitch. Brazil's third title meant they kept the Jules Rimet Trophy.

1974

West Germany

West Germany

2 goals to 1 v Netherlands, Munich

Golden BootGrzegorz Lato, Poland, 7

Johan Cruyff

Lost the final and won the Golden Ball. The Netherlands side he led changed how the game was played by every team that came after them.

1978

Argentina

Argentina

3 goals to 1 after extra time v Netherlands, Buenos Aires

Golden BootMario Kempes, Argentina, 6

Mario Kempes

Top scorer with six, two of them in the final. The tournament was staged under a military dictatorship and the questions about it have never gone away.

1982

Spain

Italy

3 goals to 1 v West Germany, Madrid

Golden BootPaolo Rossi, Italy, 6

Paolo Rossi

Six goals, weeks after a two year suspension ended. He had not scored for two years and did not score in the group stage either.

1986

Mexico

Argentina

3 goals to 2 v West Germany, Mexico City

Golden BootGary Lineker, England, 6

Diego Maradona

Five goals, including both against England four minutes apart: one with his hand, one after running past half their team. Shown here with the trophy.

1990

Italy

West Germany

1 goal to 0 v Argentina, Rome

Golden BootSalvatore Schillaci, Italy, 6

Lothar Matthäus

Captain of the last West Germany team to win anything. The country reunified three months later, and the side never played under that name again.

1994

United States

Brazil

0 goals each, 3 to 2 on penalties v Italy, Pasadena

Golden BootOleg Salenko and Hristo Stoichkov, 6

Roberto Baggio

Carried Italy to the final almost alone, then put the last penalty over the bar. The first final decided from the spot, and the first goalless one.

1998

France

France

3 goals to 0 v Brazil, Saint-Denis

Golden BootDavor Suker, Croatia, 6

Zinedine Zidane

Two headers from corners in the first half of the final, from a player who had scored twice all tournament and been sent off in the group stage.

2002

Korea and Japan

Brazil

2 goals to 0 v Germany, Yokohama

Golden BootRonaldo, Brazil, 8

Ronaldo

Eight goals and both in the final, four years after collapsing on the day of the 1998 final and two knee reconstructions after that.

2006

Germany

Italy

1 goal each, 5 to 3 on penalties v France, Berlin

Golden BootMiroslav Klose, Germany, 5

Fabio Cannavaro

Italy conceded twice all tournament, one an own goal and one a penalty. He won the Ballon d'Or that year, which defenders almost never do.

2010

South Africa

Spain

1 goal to 0 after extra time v Netherlands, Johannesburg

Golden BootFour players tied on 5

Andrés Iniesta

Scored in the 116th minute of a final that produced fourteen yellow cards. Spain's first title, and the first African tournament.

2014

Brazil

Germany

1 goal to 0 after extra time v Argentina, Rio de Janeiro

Golden BootJames Rodríguez, Colombia, 6

Mario Götze

Came off the bench and scored in the 113th minute. Germany had beaten the hosts 7 goals to 1 in the semifinal, in Brazil.

2018

Russia

France

4 goals to 2 v Croatia, Moscow

Golden BootHarry Kane, England, 6

Kylian Mbappé

Nineteen, and the second teenager after Pelé to score in a final. Croatia reached it with a population smaller than that of Paris.

2022

Qatar

Argentina

3 goals each, 4 to 2 on penalties v France, Lusail

Golden BootKylian Mbappé, France, 8

Lionel Messi

Seven goals and the Golden Ball at the fifth attempt. The first tournament played in winter, and the last of his five.

2026

Canada, Mexico and the United States

Spain

1 goal to 0 after extra time v Argentina, East Rutherford

Golden BootKylian Mbappé, France, 10

Ferran Torres

Scored early in the second period of extra time against ten men. Spain became the first country to hold the men's and women's titles at the same time.

Tournaments played

23

Twenty five were scheduled. Two were cancelled by the Second World War.

Different winners

8

Brazil five, Italy and Germany four each, Argentina three, Uruguay, France and Spain two each, England one.

Teams in 1930

13

Four of them made the boat crossing from Europe to Montevideo.

Teams in 2026

48

Across sixteen cities in three countries, with a record attendance of close to seven million.
